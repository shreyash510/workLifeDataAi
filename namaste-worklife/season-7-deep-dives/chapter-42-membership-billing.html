Chapter 42 — How Membership Billing Really Works: Contracts to Cash

Hey everyone! Welcome back to Namaste Work.Life! 🙏

In Chapter 07 we clicked "run billing" in Nexudus, and in Chapter 09 we watched invoices become revenue in NetSuite. Both treated billing as one step. It isn't. Between a signed contract and money in the bank sit about ten mechanisms: advance vs arrears, proration, deposits, booking credits, discounts, credit notes, VAT, collection, dunning and deferral. Each leaves its own footprint in the data, and each is a reason "revenue" means different numbers to different people. 💷

Many "the numbers don't match" questions on the real project will be billing questions. We take the machine apart using Pixel Chai's September in the lab, building on (not repeating) Chapter 07 and Chapter 09.

1. A Small Story First

It's 1 October. Pixel Chai's founder, Priya, opens their September statement and sees three documents: an invoice dated 1 Sep for £2,880, another dated 30 Sep for £486, and a credit of £108. "So what did September cost us?" they ask.

Meanwhile, in the same building:

  • The site manager says Pixel Chai is worth £2,400 a month (MRR).
  • Nexudus says Pixel Chai was invoiced £2,805 for September (net of VAT).
  • NetSuite says Pixel Chai produced £2,715 of revenue.
  • The bank says Pixel Chai has paid... well, it depends on the day you ask.

Every one of those numbers is correct; they answer different questions. By the end of this chapter you'll explain each pound of the difference.

2. The Billing Cycle: In Advance vs In Arrears

Coworking billing has two rhythms running at the same time:

WhatWhen billedWhyLab example
Plans and offices (recurring)In advance: at the start of the period they coverThe member is renting the space for the month. You collect before you deliver, like rent.INV-2609-01, dated 1 Sep: "Office 101 September", £2,400
Meeting room bookings, extras (usage)In arrears: after the usage, usually on the next billing runYou don't know the amount until the month has happened.INV-2609-05, dated 30 Sep: "Meeting rooms September", £405

So one company can receive two (or more) invoices about the "same" month on different dates. In the lab, all four plan/office invoices are dated 1 Sep and all four meeting room invoices are dated 30 Sep.

Things that vary between operators, and that you must not assume for Work.Life (check Nexudus docs / ask the client):

  • Whether usage is billed on a separate invoice (as in the lab) or added as extra lines to the next month's advance invoice. If it's the second, October's invoice will carry September's meeting rooms, and "invoices dated October" is not "October's activity".
  • Whether the billing day is the 1st for everyone or anchored to each contract's start date.

💡 Data rule: don't group invoices by invoice date and call it "the month". Group invoice lines by the service period they cover (or the booking date); for revenue, use NetSuite's accounting period.

3. Proration: Starting or Ending Mid-Month

Contracts don't always start on the 1st. The lab has a real example: Nomad Analytics' K004 (Unlimited Coworking, £320) started on 15 January 2025. What should the January invoice be? The lab doesn't hold January 2025 invoices, so here are the common methods, with numbers worked out by us (made up):

MethodJanuary 2025 charge for K004 (made up)Then
Calendar-day proration15–31 Jan = 17 of 31 days → 320 × 17/31 = £175.48£320 on 1 Feb, 1 Mar ...
Anchor billing to start date£320 on 15 Jan, covering 15 Jan – 14 Feb£320 on the 15th of every month
No proration (full or free first part-month)£320 or £0£320 on 1 Feb ...

Ending mid-month is the mirror image, and with billing in advance it creates a credit. Made-up example: suppose Brightline (K003, Office 103, £1,400) had left on 15 June instead of 30 June. The full £1,400 was already invoiced on 1 June. A calendar-day proration would refund 15 of 30 days:

   1 Jun   invoice   Office 103 June          + £1,400.00
   15 Jun  contract ends early (made up)
   30 Jun  credit    Office 103 16–30 June     −  £700.00   (1,400 × 15/30)
                                               ──────────
           June billed, net                      £700.00

How exactly Nexudus prorates (which basis, automatic or staff choice, part-month on its own invoice or the next) is configurable vendor behaviour: check Nexudus docs / ask the client.

⚠️ KPI trap: our fct_contract_month rule (Chapter 16) counts a contract if it's active on the last day of the month, at its full monthly price. So K004's January 2025 MRR is £320 even if the invoice was £175.48, and in the made-up Brightline case June MRR is £0 though £700 was billed. MRR is a run-rate, not a bill.

4. Deposits: Money That Isn't Yours

Many operators ask for a security deposit on offices, often a month or two of fees. The lab has no deposits, so here is a made-up one: Pixel Chai paid a £2,400 deposit (made up) when K002 started on 1 April 2026.

   Deposit received (made up)              DEBIT     CREDIT
   1000 Bank                               2,400
   2300 Deposits Held (liability)                    2,400     ← we OWE this back

   Contract ends, deposit returned in full
   2300 Deposits Held                      2,400
   1000 Bank                                         2,400

   OR: part kept against an unpaid invoice of £500 (made up)
   2300 Deposits Held                      2,400
   1100 Accounts Receivable                            500     ← clears the debt
   1000 Bank                                         1,900     ← refunded

(Account 2300 is a made-up number; the lab chart of accounts only has P&L accounts.)

A deposit never touches revenue. It sits on the balance sheet until returned or applied. A pipeline that sums "all money received" from a payments table will let deposits inflate revenue and cash KPIs. Whether a refundable deposit carries VAT, and how Nexudus records deposits (invoice line, deposit record or manual entry): check Nexudus docs / ask the client.

5. Booking Credits: How a Charge Becomes £0

Plans can include free meeting room hours (TariffBookingCredits on the plan, CoworkerBookingCredits on the member, Chapter 07). In the lab, Pixel Chai pays for every hour: their five Brick Lane bookings (9 hours at £45) make the £405 invoice. Now apply Chapter 07's exercise: Private Office includes 4 free hours a month (made up).

BookingDateHoursLab priceWith 4 free hours (made up)
B10013 Sep2£90£0 (2 credits used)
B100310 Sep2£90£0 (2 credits used, 0 left)
B100414 Sep1£45£45
B100517 Sep2£90£90
B100724 Sep2£90£90
Total9£405£225

Same 9 hours of usage, £180 less revenue. That's why meeting room revenue and utilisation are two KPIs (Chapter 07). Compute utilisation from invoice lines, or drop £0 lines in staging as "noise", and the free hours vanish.

Open details for the vendor: whether credits are consumed in date order, whether unused credits roll over or expire at month end, whether they are per member or shared by the team, and whether a £0 booking still produces an invoice line. All are check Nexudus docs / ask the client.

6. Discounts: List Price vs Contract Price

Office 101 lists at £2,600 (resources.csv). Pixel Chai pays £2,400 (K002). The discount is £200 a month, 7.7% (200 / 2,600).

A discount can be stored in at least three ways, and each looks different in the data:

How staff enter itWhat the invoice showsHow you find the discount
Price overwritten on the contract (the lab's way)One line: £2,400Compare to list price from the resource or plan
List price + discount line£2,600 and −£200Sum the discount lines
Discount code / percentage on the contractDepends on settingsRead the discount field

Which one Work.Life uses may differ by site or by who entered the deal. The KPIs it feeds: MRR uses the price actually charged (£2,400, not £2,600), average price per desk is £600 (2,400 / 4 desks) against a list £650, and discount % by site is a classic leadership question ("are we giving space away in Soho?").

7. Credit Notes

A credit note (NetSuite calls it a credit memo) reduces what a customer owes for something already invoiced. The lab's one: CN-2609-01, −£90, "Goodwill credit - AV failure 24 Sep". It matches B1007, the 2-hour Brick Lane booking on 24 Sep, account 4100 Meeting Room Revenue.

Three things to know:

  • Never delete, always credit. Once an invoice is issued, the correct fix is a credit note, not editing the invoice. So in data, "the real amount" is always invoice lines plus credit lines.
  • Where it was raised matters. In the lab the credit exists only in NetSuite, which is why Nexudus still says £3,565 (Chapter 39, I6). It could equally be raised in Nexudus (as you did in Lab 7.7) and flow to NetSuite. Which way Work.Life does it is a new question for the client.
  • Reason codes are gold. A pile of goodwill credits at one site is a service problem. If credits carry only a free-text memo, ask whether there is a reason list. (Note: fct_gl in ai_safe excludes memos, so reasons would need a separate, approved field.)

8. VAT: On the Invoice, Never in Revenue

UK standard-rate VAT is 20%. It's added to the invoice and owed to HMRC, so it's a liability, not income. The lab GL holds net amounts (VAT excluded). Pixel Chai's office invoice in full:

   INV-2609-01 (VAT added for illustration)    DEBIT     CREDIT
   1100 Accounts Receivable                    2,880                ← what Pixel Chai owes
   4000 Office Revenue                                   2,400      ← revenue (net)
   2100 VAT Payable (made-up account no.)                  480      ← owed to HMRC

Soho's September invoicing is £3,565 net, which is £4,278 gross. If someone builds a revenue tile from the invoice total column instead of the net column, Soho's revenue is overstated by exactly 20%. If a number is 1.2× finance's, look for VAT. Which items are VAT-exempt or zero-rated (if any) is for finance; don't guess.

9. Invoice → Payment → Failed Payment → Dunning → Write-off

An invoice is a claim, not cash. In general terms (the methods are typical for the industry; which ones Work.Life uses is unknown):

   INVOICE ISSUED ──► due date ──► PAID ✅  (Dr Bank / Cr AR)
        │
        └─► payment FAILS (card declined, Direct Debit bounced)
               │
               ▼
           DUNNING: reminder 1 → reminder 2 → final notice
                    (maybe: late fee, access suspended)
               │
               ├─► paid late ✅
               └─► still unpaid after N days
                      ▼
                   WRITE-OFF (bad debt):  Dr Bad Debt Expense / Cr AR
                   (or deposit applied first, see section 4)
StepWhat it meansWhat to ask
Payment methodCard on file, Direct Debit, bank transfer for larger companiesWhich payment providers does Work.Life connect to Nexudus? (Nexudus supports several; check Nexudus docs / ask the client)
Payment termsWhen it's due, e.g. on receipt or 14 days (made up)Terms per customer type?
DunningThe reminder sequence for overdue invoicesAutomated in Nexudus, done by finance, or both? Does an unpaid member lose door access?
Write-offGiving up on a debt; becomes an expenseWho approves? Which account? (UK VAT bad-debt relief rules also apply; finance handles that.)

Two data points: a write-off is an expense, not negative revenue, so September revenue doesn't change when an old invoice is written off later. And a company with unpaid invoices may still have an active contract, so it still counts in MRR. Rising overdue balances, like falling attendance, are an early churn signal.

10. Prepaid Terms and Deferred Revenue

Chapter 09 showed the mechanism with a made-up GreenRoot prepaying a year (£32,400 invoiced, £2,700 recognised a month). Two billing-specific additions:

  • Small deferrals happen every month too. If a billing run for October were dated, say, 25 September (made up), the October fees would sit in deferred revenue at 30 Sep and be released on 1 Oct. Only invoices dated within the month they cover avoid that.
  • Who does the deferral? Usually finance, in NetSuite, by journal or by NetSuite's revenue recognition features. Nexudus just records the invoice. Ask which.

Now Priya's question from the story has an answer: September cost Pixel Chai £2,715 plus VAT (£3,258 gross), of which the office was £2,400.

New questions for the client (unnumbered, to add to the list):

  • Are usage charges billed on a separate invoice or on the next month's advance invoice? What is the billing day?
  • How are mid-month starts and early exits prorated?

Interview Questions

Q: Why can a company's invoiced amount for a month differ from its MRR?

"MRR is the monthly price of contracts active at month end. Invoicing adds usage billed in arrears like meeting rooms, can be lower or higher because of proration on starts and exits, and can include one-offs or prepaid periods. In the lab, Soho's MRR is £3,040 and invoicing £3,565; the £525 is meeting rooms."

Q: A dashboard shows revenue exactly 20% above finance's number. What happened?

"Almost certainly someone used the gross invoice total including VAT. VAT is a liability owed to HMRC, not revenue. I'd switch to net line amounts, or better, to recognised revenue from the general ledger."

Q: How do booking credits affect your KPIs?

"They turn charges into £0, so meeting room revenue drops while usage doesn't. Utilisation must come from bookings, not invoice lines, and £0 bookings must not be filtered out. Revenue comes from the ledger."

Q: Is a deposit revenue? Is a written-off invoice negative revenue?

"Neither. A deposit is a liability held until it's returned or applied to a debt. A write-off is a bad debt expense; the revenue was recognised when earned and stays. Both live outside the revenue line, so they shouldn't move revenue KPIs."
