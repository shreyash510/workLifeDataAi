1. A Small Story First

Picture 2 October (illustrative). A site manager asks the AI "what did Soho make in September?" and gets £3,565, because the Nexudus invoices are all there. A week later the finance lead presents Soho's September revenue to leadership as £3,475. Same month, same site, two numbers.

Nobody made a mistake. Between those two dates, finance did the close: they posted the £90 goodwill credit memo CN-2609-01, the site-staff journals, the central cost allocations, and they reconciled what Nexudus invoiced against what NetSuite recognised. The first number was a work in progress. The second is official. The AI's job is to know the difference and to say which one it is giving.

   1 Sep ─────────── 30 Sep ───── close (WD1 … WD~7) ───── 🔒 period locked ───── board meeting
     transactions flow in           adjustments, recs,         numbers are          leadership reads

3. The Adjustments, One by One

These are the journals that make NetSuite differ from "what got invoiced" and "what got paid". Chapter 09 covered deferred revenue; here are the rest, with coworking examples.

Why it matters for us: the allocation key decides whether a site "makes money after central costs". Reading's contribution is +£450 but after its £500 share it's −£50. Change the key and Reading flips. The AI must quote the key, not just the result. New question for the client: "How are central costs allocated to sites, and how often does the key change?"

4. Reconciliations

A reconciliation proves that two records of the same thing agree, or explains exactly why they don't. The standard ones: bank (ledger vs statement: missing receipts, double payments), AR (open invoices vs the debtors account: members not paying), AP (bills vs supplier statements: missing bills), VAT (VAT wrongly posted to revenue). The one that matters most for us is Nexudus ↔ NetSuite revenue, per site:

   SOHO, SEP 2026 (lab)            NEXUDUS invoiced   NETSUITE recognised   diff
   Office 101 (Pixel Chai)                2,400              2,400            0
   Coworking (2 hot-desk members)           640                640            0
   Meeting rooms                            525                435          −90   ← CN-2609-01
                                          ─────              ─────          ───
   Total                                  3,565              3,475          −90   ✅ fully explained

   READING, SEP 2026 (lab)                2,900              2,900            0   ✅

It proves the warehouse joins the two systems correctly. In dbt it becomes a model plus a test: "unexplained difference per site per month = 0".

5. Management Accounts vs Statutory Accounts

Management accountsStatutory accounts
Who forLeadership, boardCompanies House, HMRC, shareholders
How oftenMonthlyYearly (audited if the company is above the audit thresholds)
FormatWhatever helps decisions: P&L by site, contribution, KPIsFixed by accounting standards (most UK private companies use FRS 102)
Site detailYesNo: one company (or group) total
AI scopeYesNo

⚠️ Leases are where the two can look very different. In management accounts, rent is usually a monthly cost line (our lab's account 5000). Lease accounting standards (IFRS 16, and the amended FRS 102 for periods starting on or after 1 January 2026) put most leases on the balance sheet, so "rent" turns into depreciation plus interest and EBITDA goes up. For a business whose biggest cost is rent, that matters. Ask finance which basis the board pack uses. Our assumption: management accounts with rent as a cost, which is what the lab shows.

7. Variance Analysis and Commentary

A variance is actual minus budget. For revenue and profit, negative is bad; for costs, positive is bad. Always say which way is "favourable" (F) or "adverse" (A), so nobody misreads a sign.

Sep 2026 (lab)ActualBudgetVariance%
Soho revenue£3,475£9,200−£5,725 (A)−62%
Soho direct costs£7,650£7,650£00%

Reading revenue£2,900£3,200−£300 (A)−9%

Reading contribution+£450+£750−£300 (A)−40%

The numbers say what. Commentary says why, and that's the part that today "takes someone a day to write" (Chapter 40). Good commentary names drivers you can check in the data:

SOHO — revenue £5,725 (62%) below budget; costs on budget
  Volume:  desk occupancy 17.6% (6 of 34) vs 85% budget
           • Office 102 (8 desks, list £5,000/month) empty
           • Office 103 (list £1,400/month) empty since Brightline left on 30 Jun (K003)
           • 18 of 20 hot desks unsold
  Price:   Pixel Chai pays £2,400 vs £2,600 list for Office 101 (7.7% discount, 12-month term)
  One-off: £90 goodwill credit to Pixel Chai, AV failure on 24 Sep (CN-2609-01)
  So what: costs are fixed (£7,650 whether 1 or 34 desks are sold), so every
           desk sold goes almost straight to contribution. No open Soho deals.

READING — revenue £300 (9%) below budget; contribution still positive (+£450)
  Volume:  occupancy 19% vs 20% budget; no coworking members in September
           (Kite & Key's Flex 10 moved to Soho on 1 Aug: K006 → K007, a transfer, not churn)
  Watch:   upside: Ortho Robotics, Office 202, £4,200/month quoted, tour done, wants Nov
           risk:   GreenRoot (Office 201, £2,700) renews 31 Dec; weekly attendance fell
                   73% → 23% → ~0 over September

Two rules for AI-written commentary: (1) every driver points to a row in the data (contract, GL line, booking); (2) never invent a split the data doesn't have. The lab budget is one number per site, so we can't say "the budget expected Office 102 at £5,000". We can only say the empty offices' list value (£6,400/month) is bigger than the gap.

11. Restatements: When a Closed Month Changes

Sometimes a closed month turns out to be wrong. Say (made up) that in mid-October finance finds a £300 September cleaning invoice for Soho that was never posted. There are two ways to fix it:

ApproachWhat happensEffect on September
Post in the current month (most common for small items)The £300 goes into OctoberSeptember stays −£4,175; October looks £300 worse
Reopen and restate (for material errors)Finance reopens September, posts it, locks againSeptember becomes −£4,475 (made up); the board already saw −£4,175

The second is dangerous for data: a number leadership already saw quietly changes. Two protections:

  • Snapshot what was reported. Keep an "as reported" copy of each month's KPIs at board-pack time (a dbt snapshot), so "what did we tell the board?" and "what's true now?" are both answerable.
  • Alert on closed-period changes. A test that fails if any closed month's revenue or costs move. The AI should then say: "September was restated on [date]: contribution changed from −£4,175 to −£4,475 (made up)."

The PROVISIONAL rule (Chapter 20 has the pipeline step):

  • No month named → use the last closed month.
  • Open month asked for → answer, label it PROVISIONAL, and say what usually still changes: late bills, accruals, credit memos, payroll and allocation journals.
  • Closed month restated → say so, with the old and new figure.
   "How is Soho doing this month?"   (asked 3 Oct, September still open, illustrative)

   PROVISIONAL (September not yet closed): Soho revenue so far £3,475, £5,725 below
   the £9,200 budget. Direct costs £7,650, contribution −£4,175. These may still
   change: accruals, credit memos and allocation journals are added at the close.
   Last closed month: August.

New questions for the client (unnumbered): "Are there several legal entities in NetSuite, with intercompany recharges?", "Is the board pack on a management basis with rent as a cost?", "Has a closed month ever been restated, and how is it communicated?"

Interview Questions

Q: Walk me through a month-end close.

"Cut-off first, so each item lands in the right month. Then post payroll, accruals, prepayments, deferred revenue releases, depreciation and central cost allocations, and handle intercompany if there are several entities. Then reconcile bank, debtors, creditors, VAT and, for a coworking business, membership-system invoicing against recognised revenue. Finance reviews variances, writes commentary, signs off and locks the period. Until then, numbers are provisional."

Q: What's the difference between budget, forecast and actual?

"The budget is the fixed plan made before the year. The forecast is updated regularly: actuals to date plus a best guess for the rest. Actuals come from the closed ledger. An assistant must know which one the board compares against."

Q: A closed month's number changed in the warehouse. What do you do?

"First check whether finance restated it or it's a pipeline bug. I'd keep an as-reported snapshot per board-pack month and a test that flags changes to closed periods. For a real restatement, show the new number and say it was restated, with the old figure."

Q: How would the AI explain why a site missed budget?

"Split the variance into drivers it can prove from data: occupancy (which offices and desks are empty), price (discounts against list), one-offs (credit memos) and costs against budget. Every driver must point to a contract, GL line or booking. It shouldn't invent a breakdown of the budget that the budget file doesn't contain."
