Chapter 35 — Cost: Build and Run, Leadership vs Wider Team

Hey everyone! Welcome back to Namaste Work.Life! 🙏

Brief Q4 asks for three numbers: a one-off build cost, and an ongoing annual running cost at two levels, leadership team only and wider team access. It also asks how we'd manage and cap usage and token costs. And the principles add a hard line: "This cannot become a six figure operation needing a large data team to run."

In Chapter 33 we put numbers in the response: build ~£63k, running cost ~£22k a year for the leadership team and ~£42.5k a year for wider team access. In this chapter we show exactly how we got them, so you can rebuild the model yourself, change any assumption, and defend every number on the client call.

The split with Chapter 27 is deliberate: Chapter 27 works out what one question costs in tokens and bytes, and the caps; this chapter uses exactly the same usage assumptions and per-question figures and adds build effort, support and hosting.

⚠️ Every figure in this chapter is an estimate. Vendor prices change often. The token prices and the day rate are made up for this tutorial. Before sending anything to a client, check current pricing on each vendor's official page and use your own team's rates.

1. A Small Story First

A leader reads "AI" in a proposal and imagines a giant bill. 💸 Then they see our running cost breakdown for the leadership team: the AI model itself is around £2,500 a year, and most of that is the accuracy test runs, not the leaders' questions. BigQuery is a few hundred pounds. The biggest line by far is people: someone keeping it healthy, adding KPIs, and checking accuracy.

That surprises most clients, and it's the most useful thing to explain. Work.Life has ~8,000 members and 14 sites. That's small data. The warehouse tables have thousands or a few million rows. The cost risk isn't the data. It's an uncapped AI service, or a system so complex it needs a data team. Our design avoids both.

5. Running Cost 1: AI Model Tokens

Tokens are the units AI models charge for, roughly ¾ of a word each. Chapter 27 worked out one question at ≈ £0.03 (illustrative price). Now scale it up, step by step:

   ONE MONTH (estimates, illustrative prices)       LEADERSHIP      WIDER TEAM

   Questions             10 × 8 × 21                    1,680
                         70 × 3 × 21                                    4,410
   Question tokens       × £0.03                      ≈ £ 50          ≈ £130
   Digests & alerts      narration only               ≈ £  5          ≈ £ 10
   Golden test runs      ~5,100 × £0.03 (Ch 27)       ≈ £150          ≈ £150
                                                        ─────           ─────
                                                      ≈ £205/month    ≈ £290/month
                                                      ≈ £2,500/year   ≈ £3,500/year

Notice the biggest token line is testing, not leaders asking questions. That's the Chapter 27 surprise, and it's the right way round: the accuracy gate is what makes the answers trustworthy. The test schedule (nightly tier 1, weekly full set, full set on every change) is what keeps it at ~£150 instead of ~£540 for nightly full runs.

Stress test: if the real model costs 5× the illustrative price, leadership tokens become ≈ £1,000/month (≈ £12,000/year) and the wider team ≈ £1,450/month (≈ £17,500/year). Still nowhere near six figures, and the caps in section 9 stop it going further. Check current pricing before quoting any of this.

Wider-team questions are more varied and will hit more refusals and retries, which is one reason the support line doubles (section 7).

6. Running Cost 2: BigQuery, Hosting and the Rest

BigQuery charges on-demand queries by data scanned (published at about $6.25 per TiB at the time of writing, with the first 1 TiB per month free), and storage per GiB per month (with a small free allowance). Check current pricing and region.

                    only needed columns selected
   Scanned          3,360 × 20 MB                      ≈  67 GB   (same as Chapter 27)
                                                         < 1 TiB free tier
   Query cost                                          ≈  £0
   Storage          ai_safe copy: a few GB             ≈  £0 – a few £

So why budget £500/year (leadership) and £1,000/year (wider team)? For the daily refresh of the ai_safe copy, development and test environments, golden test runs, and the free tier being shared with everything else in Work.Life's billing account. The real BigQuery risk isn't volume; it's one badly written query scanning a huge table. That's why every AI query gets a maximum bytes billed limit (Chapter 27).

Hosting, scheduling and logging (for example, a small container service such as Cloud Run in Work.Life's Google Cloud, a sign-in proxy, a scheduler and a small database for the answer log): a chat app for 10 people sits mostly inside free allowances, but we budget £1,000/year for leadership and £2,000/year for the wider team to cover monitoring, log storage and spare capacity.

Interview Questions

Q: How do you estimate LLM running costs for a new internal tool?

"Users × questions per day × working days gives questions per month. Multiply by calls per question and tokens per call, split input and output, and apply the current per-million prices. Add test runs (often the biggest token line) and scheduled jobs. Stress-test at several times the price, and put hard caps on top."

Q: Why is BigQuery cost so low here?

"The data is small: thousands to a few million rows. The AI queries a compact, partitioned copy and selects only the needed columns, so monthly scanned data stays within or near the free tier. The real risk is a single huge scan, which a maximum-bytes-billed limit prevents."

Q: What's usually the biggest ongoing cost of an AI analytics tool, and how do you reduce it?

"People: support, definition changes and accuracy checks. You reduce it with good documentation, a stable semantic layer, automated golden tests, and clear in-house owners for definitions, so the supplier isn't needed for every small change."

Q: A client asks, "What stops the AI bill from exploding?"

"Per-user limits, a hard monthly cap at the provider with early alerts, query byte limits in BigQuery, cloud budget alerts, caching, and using cheaper models where we can. If the cap is reached, the service pauses and tells you. It can't bill more."
