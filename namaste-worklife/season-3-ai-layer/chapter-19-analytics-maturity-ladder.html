1. A Small Story First

A leadership meeting, first Monday of October. Someone opens the Tableau dashboard. Soho revenue for September: £3,475. Budget: £9,200.

Then the room goes quiet, and three questions follow in a row:

  • "Why is it so far below budget?"
  • "Is it going to get worse?"
  • "What should we do?"

The dashboard can't answer any of them. Someone says "I'll dig into it and come back to you", and a week later a spreadsheet arrives. By then it's mid-October and the next month has nearly closed. That's the brief's pain in one scene: "Insight is slow and laborious to produce."

Each of those three questions sits on a different rung of the ladder. The dashboard lives on the first rung. The questions live on rungs two, three and four.

RungQuestionTypical toolsHard part

💡 Each rung uses the rungs below it. You can't explain why revenue fell if you can't say what revenue was. You can't forecast churn if you can't diagnose past churn.

4. One Question, Five Rungs: "How Is Soho Doing?"

Let's take the lab's Soho site in September 2026 and climb the ladder with it. Every number below comes from lab-data/ and matches the Chapter 11 exam answers.

   RUNG 1 · DESCRIPTIVE   "What happened?"
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Soho, Sep 2026:  recognised revenue  £3,475   (budget £9,200)
                    direct costs        £7,650   (budget £7,650)
                    contribution       −£4,175
                    desk occupancy       17.6%   (budget 85%)

   RUNG 2 · DIAGNOSTIC    "Why?"
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   • Office 102 (8 people, £5,000/month list) has been empty all year
   • Office 103 (£1,400/month) empty since Brightline left on 30 Jun
     (lost reason: "Moving to hybrid; downsizing to home working")
   • 18 of 20 hot desks unsold
   • Costs are fixed and on budget, so the gap is all revenue

   RUNG 3 · PREDICTIVE    "What will happen?"
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   • Pixel Chai (Office 101) is contracted to Mar 2027, so that's safe
   • No open Soho pipeline in the CRM, so no new revenue is visible
   → Without action, Soho stays around −£4k/month contribution

   RUNG 4 · PRESCRIPTIVE  "What should we do?"
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Options for humans to weigh (not for the AI to decide):
   • Push Office 102 and 103 to brokers; review the £5,000 list price
   • Market the hot desks; test a Soho-only offer
   • Review whether Office 102 could be split into smaller offices

   RUNG 5 · ACTIVATION    "Act on it"
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   • Monday alert: "Soho September revenue 62% below budget"
     (£3,475 vs £9,200; the lab only has September, so no trend yet)
   • Auto-create a CRM task for sales when an office is empty 60+ days

Look at what each rung needed. Rung 1 is a single query on fct_site_month_kpis. Rung 2 needed four systems: Nexudus (contracts, resources), NetSuite (costs), Excel (budget, capacity) and the CRM (lost reason). That's why diagnostic questions are hard today, and why the AI must sit on the warehouse.

⚠️ Be honest about rung 4. The brief's own example question ends with "and what could we do about it". An AI can list the drivers and suggest options from the data. It cannot know things that live only in leaders' heads or in documents it was never given, for example a lease break date or a conversation with a broker. In our response we say: Phase 1 informs decisions, it doesn't make them. The brief explicitly asks for "honest feedback on what is and is not realistic".

7. Why You Can't Skip Rungs

It's tempting to jump straight to the exciting rungs. Here's what goes wrong each time:

Skip……straight toWhat breaks
Agreed definitions (rung 1)An AI chatbot (rung 2)It answers "occupancy" three different ways. Trust is gone in one meeting.
Diagnosing past churn (rung 2)Churn prediction (rung 3)The model learns from data where upgrades and transfers count as churn (Chapter 02). Garbage in, garbage out.
Accurate predictions (rung 3)Automated actions (rung 5)The system auto-emails a "we'll miss you" offer to a member who is upgrading.

This is why Phase 1 spends so much effort on rung 1, through the semantic layer (Chapter 21) and the accuracy gate (Chapter 25), even though the brief already says descriptive is "in place". An AI layer amplifies whatever the definitions are. If they're inconsistent, it amplifies the inconsistency.

8. What "Trust" Means on Each Rung

The brief says "trust the answers". Trust looks different on each rung, so it's tested differently:

RungA trustworthy answer…How we test it
Descriptive…matches the board pack to the poundGolden questions vs closed-month actuals (Ch 25)
Diagnostic…names the real drivers and shows its workingsExpert review: would Finance and Ops agree?
Predictive…gives a range, not a single confident numberBack-testing on past months (Phase 2)
Prescriptive…offers options and says what it doesn't knowLeadership feedback, not a pass/fail test
Activation…fires rarely, and is right when it doesAlert precision: how many alerts led to action? (Ch 24)

Interview Questions

Q: Explain the analytics maturity ladder to a non-technical executive.

"Five questions in order: what happened, why, what will happen, what should we do, and can the system act for us. Each step needs the ones below it. Most companies have the first step as dashboards. The biggest everyday gain is usually the second step, getting answers to 'why' quickly without waiting for an analyst."

Q: Work.Life says descriptive reporting is already in place. Why would you still invest in it in an AI project?

"Because an AI layer reuses those definitions for every answer. If occupancy or revenue are defined inconsistently, the AI will be confidently inconsistent. Phase 1 has to make the descriptive layer one governed set of definitions, which then also makes the dashboards better."

Q: Can an AI answer 'what should we do about Soho?'

"It can show the drivers, for example empty offices and unsold hot desks against fixed costs, and list sensible options from the data. It can't know the commercial context only leaders have, like lease terms or negotiations. I'd position it as decision support, and say that clearly to the client."

Q: Which rung would you build first for this client, and why?

"Diagnostic, on top of a hardened descriptive layer. It's their stated priority and their biggest gap, it reuses the warehouse they already have, and it creates the trusted definitions that prediction and automation in Phase 2 depend on."
