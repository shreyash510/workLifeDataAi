Business Guide 02 — A Tour of the Dashboards

Hi again! 🙏 In Guide 01 you met the dashboards by name. Now we walk through each one: what is on it, how to pick a site and a month, how to read it in 30 seconds, and what warning signs to look for. You do not need any technical knowledge for this.

⚠️ About the examples. Every number here comes from Mini Work.Life, our made-up two-site company (Soho and Reading), for September 2026. The layouts are our standard set (🛠). Your real dashboards may look a little different, and the dashboard tool itself may change: the scoping brief says the current tool is under review (✅ confirmed, brief).

1. Things Every Dashboard Has

Every dashboard has the same strip across the top. Learn it once and you can read all of them.

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Site: [All sites ▼]   Month: [Sep 2026 ▼]     Last updated: 06:10    │
│ ⚠ PROVISIONAL: this month is not closed yet (shown only if open)     │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
PartWhat it doesWhat to check
Site filterPick one site or "All sites". Everything on the page changes to match.Make sure it says what you think. A common mistake is reading "Soho" numbers while thinking it is the whole company.
Month filterPick one month, or a range of months.If you pick a range, check every month in it has finance numbers (see the blank warning below).
Last updatedWhen the data was last copied in from your systems (each night, 🛠 our standard).If it shows yesterday's date or older, the nightly copy did not run. Tell the data owner before you use the numbers.
Provisional labelShows when the month you picked is still open, which means finance has not closed it.Do not quote a provisional profit as final. Guide 04 explains why.

💡 Blank is not zero. If finance has not yet posted a month's numbers, revenue and cost boxes show blank (or "no data yet"), never $0. In Mini Work.Life, finance numbers exist only for September. If you choose July to September, the revenue total shows $7,968.75, which is September alone. July and August are blank, not zero. Never read that as "the quarter's revenue was $7,968.75".

2. Leadership Overview

What it is for: one page that tells leadership how the company and each site did in a month.

LEADERSHIP OVERVIEW            Site: All sites   Month: Sep 2026
┌────────────┬────────────┬──────────────┬────────────┬──────────────┐
│ MRR        │ Revenue    │ Contribution │ Desk occ.  │ vs Budget    │
│ $7,175     │ $7,968.75     │ −$4,656.25      │ 18.5%      │ −$7,531.25      │
│            │            │              │ 12 of 65   │ (budget      │
│            │            │              │            │  $15,500)    │
└────────────┴────────────┴──────────────┴────────────┴──────────────┘
 SITE TABLE
 Site     MRR      Revenue   Contribution   Desk occ.       vs Budget
 Soho     $3,800   $4,343.75    −$5,218.75        17.6% (6/34)    −$7,156.25
 Reading  $3,375   $3,625    +$562.50          19.4% (6/31)    −$375
 [ chart: MRR by month, last 12 months ]

Read it in 30 seconds:

  1. Check the top strip: right month, "last updated" is today, provisional or not.
  2. Look at the five tiles. Is contribution (profit after site costs) above or below zero?
  3. Look at the "vs Budget" column. Which site is furthest off plan?
  4. Look at the MRR chart. Is recurring income going up or down?

Worked example (made up): In September, Mini Work.Life brought in $7,968.75 of revenue against a $15,500 budget, so it is $7,531.25 below plan. Almost all of the gap is Soho: $7,156.25 below its $11,500 budget (−62%). Reading is close to plan (−$375) and makes +$562.50 at site level. Soho has 6 of 34 desks filled (17.6%). The story for the meeting: "Soho is the problem; Reading is roughly on plan."

Warning signs: desk occupancy that does not match what the site team sees on the floor; a big jump in MRR with no new contracts you know of; a revenue tile that is blank (finance has not posted that month yet).

3. Site P&L

What it is for: the profit and loss for one site, line by line. "P&L" just means money in, money out, and what is left.

SITE P&L                       Site: Soho   Month: Sep 2026
 REVENUE                         Actual      Budget
   Office                        $3,000
   Coworking                       $800
   Meeting rooms                   $543.75   ($656.25 billed − $112.50 credit note)
   Total revenue                 $4,343.75      $11,500    → −$7,156.25 (−62%)
 DIRECT COSTS (site costs)
   Rent $5,625 · Rates $1,125 · Service charge $500
   Utilities $437.50 · Site staff $1,500 · Cleaning $375
   Total direct costs            $9,562.50      $9,562.50
 CONTRIBUTION                   −$5,218.75
 Share of central costs          $1,250
 AFTER CENTRAL COSTS            −$6,468.75

Read it in 30 seconds: total revenue, total direct costs, then contribution. Then compare revenue with budget. Costs are often on plan; revenue is usually where the gap is.

Worked example (made up): Soho's costs are exactly on budget ($9,562.50). The whole problem is revenue: $4,343.75 against $11,500. The budget expected 85% of desks filled; only 17.6% are. So the question for the site team is about selling desks, not cutting costs.

Warning signs: a line that is much bigger or smaller than last month (a missing bill, or a bill posted to the wrong site); a credit note you did not expect; "after central costs" being used to judge a site. Central costs are a share of head office costs, and the share depends on how finance splits them. Judge a site on contribution first (Guide 03).

4. Members & Retention

What it is for: who your members are, who joined, who left, who moved, and why.

MEMBERS & RETENTION            Site: All sites   Month: Sep 2026
┌──────────────┬────────────────┬──────────────────┬───────────────┐
│ Members      │ Member         │ Companies lost   │ Net member    │
│ 12           │ companies 4    │ in 2026: 1       │ movement 2026 │
│              │                │                  │ +8            │
└──────────────┴────────────────┴──────────────────┴───────────────┘
 MOVEMENTS IN 2026          Type        MRR change
   GreenRoot (Jan)          New         +$3,375
   Kite & Key (Feb)         New           +$225
   Pixel Chai (Apr)         Upgrade     +$2,600
   Brightline (Jun)         Churn       −$1,750   reason: moving to hybrid
   Kite & Key (Aug)         Transfer      +$175   Reading → Soho

Read it in 30 seconds: member count, companies lost, then the movements list. For every churn line, read the reason.

Worked example (made up): Only one company left in 2026: Brightline, 2 people, $1,750 of monthly income, "moving to hybrid". Pixel Chai moving from a hot desk to an office is an upgrade, and Kite & Key moving from Reading to Soho is a transfer. Neither is churn, because the company stayed with us (Guide 03 explains each word).

Warning signs: members counted at the "wrong" site. Mini Work.Life has 6 members at each site by contract site, but 5 at Soho and 7 at Reading by home site, because Kite & Key's home site is Reading while its contract is at Soho. Always check which one the dashboard uses. Also watch for a churn with no reason entered.

5. Meeting Rooms

What it is for: how busy each room is, and what rooms earn.

MEETING ROOMS                  Site: All sites   Month: Sep 2026
 Room                 Hours booked   Hours open   Use     Billed
 Brick Lane (Soho)    9              220          4.1%    $506.25
 Carnaby (Soho)       4              220          1.8%    $150
 Thames (Reading)     5              220          2.3%    $250
 Total                18             660          2.7%    $906.25
 [ chart: bookings by weekday and hour ]

Read it in 30 seconds: the "Use" column first (hours booked ÷ hours the room is open), then the busiest days and times in the chart.

Worked example (made up): September 2026 has 22 working days. At 10 open hours a day, each room has 220 hours. Brick Lane had 5 bookings, 9 hours in all, so 9 ÷ 220 = 4.1%. Most of those are Pixel Chai's regular Thursday morning bookings. Real rooms are much busier; our lab is a sleepy one.

Warning signs: "Billed" here is what was booked and charged at the time. It is not the same as revenue in the accounts. Soho rooms billed $656.25, but a $112.50 goodwill credit note (for broken screen equipment on 24 September) brings the revenue down to $543.75. Use the Site P&L for revenue. Also check the room's opening hours are right, because they set the "hours open" number. ❓ Your real opening hours come from the site team.

6. Sales Pipeline

What it is for: open deals, upcoming renewals, and how many deals you win.

SALES PIPELINE                 Site: All sites   As of: 30 Sep 2026
┌──────────────────┬──────────────┬─────────────────┐
│ Open pipeline    │ Win rate     │ Discount, last  │
│ $8,750 / month   │ 60% (3 of 5) │ won deal: 7.7%  │
└──────────────────┴──────────────┴─────────────────┘
 OPEN DEALS                  Stage           $/month   Expected close
   Ortho Robotics, Office 202  Tour done       $5,250    31 Oct
   GreenRoot renewal, O201     Proposal sent   $3,500    30 Nov

Read it in 30 seconds: total open pipeline, then each deal's stage and expected close date. Then the win rate.

Worked example (made up): Open deals are worth $8,750 a month: GreenRoot's renewal at $3,500 (their contract ends 31 December) plus Ortho Robotics at $5,250. Of the 5 deals closed so far, 3 were won, so the win rate is 60%. Pixel Chai's office was won at $3,000 against a $3,250 list price, a 7.7% discount.

Warning signs: deals with an expected close date in the past that are still "open" (the sales system has not been updated); a renewal that is not in the pipeline at all. Pipeline value is a hope, not income. Never add it to MRR.

7. Attendance (Only If Agreed)

What it is for: how often each member company actually comes in. It uses door-access data, which is personal data, so it only exists if your company has agreed it. It shows company-level totals only, for groups of 5 or more people (🛠 our standard). You will never see a single person's badge-ins.

ATTENDANCE                     Site: Reading   Month: Sep 2026
 Company     People   Wk 1   Wk 2   Wk 3   Wk 4
 GreenRoot   6        73%    40%    23%    ~0%
 (companies with fewer than 5 people are not shown)

Worked example (made up): GreenRoot's weekly badge-in rate fell from 73% to about zero over September. Their contract ends on 31 December and the renewal is still at "proposal sent". That is an early warning: the account manager should talk to them now, not in December.

Warning signs: a company with fewer than 5 people showing on the page (tell the data owner at once); low numbers caused by a broken door reader rather than real absence.

8. Exporting and Sharing Safely

DoDo not
Share a link to the dashboard with colleagues who have access.Send exports or screenshots outside the company.
Export a site or company total for a meeting pack.Include member names, emails or any personal data in screenshots.
Write the month and "provisional" on any export from an open month.Paste numbers into your own spreadsheet and change them there.
Note the "last updated" time on anything you send.Forward attendance data beyond the people who agreed to see it.

💡 An export is a copy frozen in time. The dashboard keeps updating each night; your export does not. Always put the date on it.

Quick Check

1. You pick July to September and revenue shows $7,968.75. What do you check?

Whether every month has finance numbers. In Mini Work.Life, July and August are blank, so $7,968.75 is September only.

2. "Last updated" shows yesterday morning. What does that mean?

The nightly copy did not run. Tell the data owner before using the numbers.

3. Which dashboard do you use for meeting room revenue, and why?

The Site P&L. The Meeting Rooms dashboard shows what was billed ($656.25 at Soho), but revenue after the $112.50 credit note is $543.75.

4. Is Pixel Chai moving to an office a loss of a member?

No. It is an upgrade. The company stayed and pays more.

5. Can you put a screenshot of the Members dashboard with member names in a supplier email?

No. No sharing outside the company, and no personal data in screenshots.
