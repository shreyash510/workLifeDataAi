Business Guide 03 — What Every Number Means

Hi again! 🙏 Two people can look at the same dashboard and mean different things by "revenue" or "occupancy". This guide fixes that. For every number you will see what it means, how it is worked out in words, a Mini Work.Life example, and the most common mistake.

⚠️ About the examples. All numbers are from Mini Work.Life (made up), September 2026 unless it says otherwise. The definitions are our standard (🛠). Your company may agree small changes; if so, the agreed version is written down once and every dashboard uses it.

What we will cover:

  • Space numbers: capacity, desk occupancy, office occupancy
  • Money numbers: MRR, revenue, budget and variance, costs, contribution
  • Member numbers: members, companies, the six movement types, net movement
  • Meeting room use
  • Sales numbers: pipeline, win rate, discount
  • Same word, different meaning

1. Space Numbers

NumberWhat it means and how it is worked outMini Work.LifeCommon mistake
Capacity (sellable desks)How many desks a site can sell: desks in offices plus hot desks. It comes from the capacity sheet the business keeps up to date.Soho: 14 office desks + 20 hot desks = 34. Reading: 16 + 15 = 31. Total 65.Forgetting to update it when a room is turned into an office or a floor is closed. Every occupancy number then goes wrong.
Desk occupancyDesks filled by a contract on the last day of the month, divided by sellable desks.Soho 6 ÷ 34 = 17.6%. Reading 6 ÷ 31 = 19.4%. All sites 12 ÷ 65 = 18.5%.Averaging the sites' percentages (see the box below).
Office occupancyPrivate offices let, divided by private offices you have. Counts rooms, not desks.Soho 1 of 3 offices (Office 101). Reading 1 of 2 (Office 201).Mixing it up with desk occupancy. A site can have most offices let but many empty hot desks.

⚠️ Add up desks first, then divide. Never average two sites' percentages. With our two similar-sized sites the error is tiny (18.5% either way, rounded). But picture a big site with 300 desks at 90% and a small one with 20 desks at 20% (made up). Average of the two percentages: 55%. The true answer: (270 + 4) ÷ 320 = 85.6%. Averaging gives the small site the same weight as the big one.

2. Money Numbers

NumberWhat it means and how it is worked outMini Work.LifeCommon mistake
MRR (monthly recurring revenue)The monthly price of every contract active on the last day of the month, added up. It is a "promise" number: what contracts say members pay each month. It leaves out one-off items like meeting room bookings.Soho £3,040 (Pixel Chai office £2,400 + two hot desks at £320). Reading £2,700 (GreenRoot office). Total £5,740.Calling MRR "revenue". It is not what finance puts in the accounts.
RevenueWhat finance counts as earned in the month in the accounts (NetSuite). It includes meeting rooms and takes off credit notes.Soho £3,475, Reading £2,900. Total £6,375.Using invoices from Nexudus as revenue (see "invoiced" below).
Invoiced vs recognised"Invoiced" is what was billed. "Recognised" is what the accounts count as earned for the month. A credit note is a refund or discount given after an invoice; it lowers revenue.Soho invoiced £3,565. A £90 goodwill credit note (screen equipment failed on 24 September) brings recognised revenue to £3,475.Thinking the two systems disagree. Often both are right; the difference is explained by credit notes and timing (Guide 04).
BudgetThe plan for the year, set before it starts. It sits in the business's Excel sheet.September revenue budget: Soho £9,200, Reading £3,200. Total £12,400.Mixing up budget (fixed plan) with forecast (a newer best guess). ❓ Ask your finance team which one the board pack uses.
Variance (vs budget)Actual minus budget. Negative means below plan. As a percentage: variance ÷ budget.Soho £3,475 − £9,200 = −£5,725 (−62%). Reading −£300. All sites −£6,025.Reading a negative cost variance as bad news. For costs, below budget is good; for revenue, below budget is bad.
Direct costs (site costs)Costs a site causes: rent, business rates, service charge, utilities, site staff, cleaning.Soho £7,650 (rent alone is £4,500). Reading £2,450.Leaving out a late bill, so a site looks better than it is until the month closes.
ContributionRevenue minus direct costs. What a site adds to (or takes from) the company before head office costs. The best number for judging a site.Soho £3,475 − £7,650 = −£4,175. Reading £2,900 − £2,450 = +£450. All sites −£3,725.Calling it "profit". It is profit at site level only.
Central costsHead office costs (finance, sales, IT, leadership) that no single site causes. Finance gives each site a share.Soho £1,000, Reading £500.Thinking closing a site removes its share. The share just moves to the other sites.
Contribution after central costsContribution minus the site's share of central costs.Soho −£5,175. Reading −£50.Calling Reading "loss-making" without saying it makes +£450 before central costs, and that −£50 depends on how central costs are split.

3. Member Numbers

NumberWhat it meansMini Work.LifeCommon mistake
MembersPeople with an active membership at the month end.12 members: 6 at Soho and 6 at Reading by contract site.Not saying which site rule you use. By the company's home site it is 5 at Soho and 7 at Reading, because Kite & Key's home site is Reading but its contract is at Soho.
Member companiesCompanies with at least one active contract.4: Pixel Chai, Nomad Analytics, Kite & Key, GreenRoot.Mixing up "we lost a member" (a person) with "we lost a member company" (a whole account).

The six movement types. Every change to a contract is one of these:

TypeWhat it meansMini Work.Life, 2026
NewA company starts its first contract.GreenRoot, January, office £2,700. Kite & Key, February, Flex 10 Days £180.
UpgradeThe same company moves to something bigger or pricier.Pixel Chai, April: hot desk £320 → Office 101 £2,400 (+£2,080 a month).
DowngradeThe same company moves to something smaller or cheaper.None in 2026.
TransferThe same company moves to another site.Kite & Key, August: Reading Flex 10 → Soho hot desk (+£140 a month).
RenewalThe same company signs again when its term ends.None yet. GreenRoot's renewal (ends 31 December) is still open.
ChurnA company leaves and does not come back.Brightline, ended 30 June: 2 people, £1,400 a month, "moving to hybrid".

💡 Why an upgrade or a transfer is not churn. In both, one contract ends and another starts. A careless count sees "a contract ended" and calls it a leaver. But the company never left; it is paying us more, or just at another site. So in 2026 Mini Work.Life lost one company (Brightline), not three.

NumberWhat it means and how it is worked outMini Work.LifeCommon mistake
Net member movementMembers who joined minus members who left, over a period, leaving out moves inside the company (upgrades, transfers).January to September 2026: 10 joined (6 GreenRoot, 1 Kite & Key, 3 more at Pixel Chai), 2 left (Brightline) = +8. 4 members on 1 January, 12 on 30 September.Counting a transfer as one leaver at one site and one joiner at another, which inflates both numbers.

4. Meeting Room Use

NumberWhat it means and how it is worked outMini Work.LifeCommon mistake
Room use (utilisation)Hours booked ÷ hours the room is open in the month.Brick Lane: 9 hours booked. September has 22 working days × 10 open hours = 220. 9 ÷ 220 = 4.1%.Using the wrong opening hours. ❓ The site team owns the real opening hours.
Meeting room revenueRoom bookings billed, minus any credit notes, as counted in the accounts.Soho: £525 billed − £90 credit note = £435. Reading £200.Using the booking total as revenue.

5. Sales Numbers

NumberWhat it means and how it is worked outMini Work.LifeCommon mistake
Pipeline valueThe monthly price of all deals still open, added up.£7,000 a month: GreenRoot renewal £2,800 + Ortho Robotics £4,200.Adding it to MRR or the forecast as if it were won.
Win rateDeals won ÷ deals closed (won plus lost). Open deals are left out.3 won of 5 closed = 60%.Dividing by all deals, including open ones, which makes the rate look worse.
Discount(List price − price agreed) ÷ list price.Pixel Chai: (£2,600 − £2,400) ÷ £2,600 = 7.7%.Looking at the price alone. A deal can look fine while giving away a lot against list.

6. Same Word, Different Meaning

WordCan meanWhat the dashboards mean
RevenueMRR, invoices billed, or revenue in the accountsRevenue in the accounts (after credit notes). MRR is always called MRR.
OccupancyDesks, offices, or floor area; month end or month averageDesks filled on the last day of the month ÷ sellable desks. Office occupancy is shown separately.
MemberA person, or a companyA person. A company is a "member company".
ChurnAny contract ending, or a company leavingA company leaving. Upgrades, downgrades and transfers are not churn.
SiteWhere the contract is, or the company's home siteWhere the contract is (the dashboard says so if it uses home site).
ProfitContribution, after central costs, or the company's bottom lineThe dashboards say "contribution" or "after central costs", never just "profit".
BudgetThe fixed annual plan, or the latest forecastThe fixed annual plan. ❓ Ask your finance team if a forecast is also shown.
PipelineOpen deals, or all deals everOpen deals only, at their monthly value.

Quick Check

1. Soho is 17.6% full and Reading 19.4%. Why not just average them?

Because sites have different numbers of desks. Add desks filled (12) and sellable desks (65), then divide: 18.5%.

2. Soho invoiced £3,565 but revenue is £3,475. Which is wrong?

Neither. A £90 credit note was given after the invoice, so recognised revenue is £90 lower.

3. Kite & Key moved from Reading to Soho. Is that churn?

No. It is a transfer. The company stayed.

4. Is Reading making money?

Yes at site level (+£450 contribution). After its £500 share of central costs it is −£50, and that depends on how central costs are split.

5. Open pipeline is £7,000 a month. Can you add it to MRR?

No. Pipeline is deals not yet won. Only signed contracts count in MRR.
