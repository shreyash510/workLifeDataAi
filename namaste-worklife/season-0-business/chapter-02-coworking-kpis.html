1. A Small Story First

An imaginary Monday leadership meeting (made-up numbers). The ops lead says "Soho is 92% occupied." The finance lead says "Soho is 78% occupied." Both are looking at "occupancy". Both are right. 😅

  • Ops counted offices: 23 of 25 offices have someone in them = 92%.
  • Finance counted desks: 2 big offices are sold at a discount to small teams, so of 300 desks only 234 are paid for = 78%.

The meeting turns into an argument about numbers instead of a decision about the business. This is exactly the problem in the brief: "it should understand our board pack, our KPIs, why those KPIs matter." An AI that picks the wrong definition is worse than no AI.

3. Occupancy: The King KPI

There are three common ways to calculate it. Know all three.

   1) OFFICE OCCUPANCY (by count)
      occupied offices / total offices
      23 / 25 = 92%

   2) DESK OCCUPANCY (by workstation)  ← most common in flex industry
      desks under contract / total desks
      234 / 300 = 78%

   3) AREA OCCUPANCY (by sq ft)
      sq ft under contract / sellable sq ft
      9,800 / 12,000 = 82%

And then there's physical occupancy: how many people actually came in (from key card / access logs). A site can be 95% contracted but only 40% physically used on a Friday. That second number predicts churn: teams who never come in tend to leave at renewal. 🔮

TypeAnswersSource
Contracted occupancyHow much are we selling?Nexudus contracts
Physical occupancyHow much is it actually used?Door-access events (for example ACT365, if that's what "ACT" is) or Nexudus check-ins

⚠️ Trap: MRR and invoices in Nexudus (what is contracted and billed) ≠ revenue in NetSuite (what accountants recognised). Credit notes, prepayments, deposits and month-end adjustments make them differ. For "is the site profitable", finance's number wins. For "how many members on which plan", Nexudus wins. 5. Member KPIs

   MEMBERS AT START OF MONTH      1,000
 + NEW MEMBERS JOINED              + 60
 − MEMBERS LEFT (churned)          − 45
                                  ──────
   MEMBERS AT END OF MONTH        1,015

   NET MEMBER MOVEMENT = 60 − 45 = +15
   MONTHLY CHURN RATE  = 45 / 1,000 = 4.5%

"Net member movements" is named in the brief's Phase 2 list: "resident revenue projection, net member movements, seasonality and meeting room demand". Now you know what it is.

KPIFormulaWatch out for
Logo churnCompanies lost / companies at startA 20-person team leaving = 1 logo
Desk churnDesks lost / desks at startBetter reflects revenue impact
Revenue churnMRR lost / MRR at startThe most honest one
Net revenue retention(Start MRR − churn − downgrades + upgrades) / start MRR>100% means existing members grow
Renewal rateContracts renewed / contracts that reached end dateOnly counts contracts that could renew
Average tenureAverage months a member staysLong tenure = loyal base

Moves inside the business are not churn. If Priya moves from a hot desk to a private office, that's an upgrade. If a team moves from Soho to Fitzrovia, that's a transfer. A naive query counts both as "1 left + 1 joined". Defining these properly is a big part of the job.

6. Meeting Room Utilisation

   UTILISATION = hours booked / hours available

   A busy 8-person room (made-up numbers)
   Available: 10 hours/day × 22 working days = 220 hours
   Booked:    132 hours
   Utilisation = 132 / 220 = 60%

In our lab (Chapter 07) the Soho room "Brick Lane" has only 9 hours booked in September: 9 / 220 = 4.1%.

Split it two ways: paid vs free (members often get free credits on their plan) and by hour of day (10am–3pm are full, 5pm is empty). This is the "meeting room demand" forecasting from Phase 2.

When someone asks "is Soho profitable?", ask back (or make the AI ask back): before or after central costs? The answer can flip from yes to no. That's why "business context" in the brief is so important.

Interview Questions

Q: Two people report different occupancy for the same site. How do you fix it?

"Usually they're using different definitions: offices vs desks vs area, or a snapshot date vs a monthly average. I'd agree one official definition with the business, write it down with formula, source and grain, implement it once in the modelling layer, and have every dashboard and the AI use that single model."

Q: What's the difference between MRR in the membership system and revenue in the finance system?

"MRR is the contracted monthly value from active contracts. Recognised revenue is what accounting posts to the ledger, which includes credit notes, deferrals, adjustments and one-off income. They should be close, but finance is the source of truth for profitability and the membership system is the source of truth for member counts and contracts."

Q: How would you calculate churn so upgrades and site transfers aren't counted as churn?

"Measure at the member or company level, not the contract level. If a company's contract ends but they have another active contract starting within a short window, at any site, it's a move, not churn. I'd classify each contract end as churn, upgrade, downgrade or transfer in a dbt model."

Q: Give an example of a leading indicator for churn.

"Falling physical usage from access logs, fewer meeting room bookings, support complaints, or a contract approaching its end date with no renewal conversation logged in the CRM."
